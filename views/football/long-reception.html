              Method

              1. Where it comes from. nflverse play-by-play for 2024–2026, reduced to every completed reception with its receiver, yardage and defense — and every target with its air yards, which is where depth comes from. The prices are a DraftKings capture: this market is in no odds API — balldontlie carries 18 NFL prop types and this is not one of them. The receptions market is, and that one is read through the API for every game on the slate.
              2. Per-catch explosiveness. Catch-yardage survival S(x) is measured, not assumed — its tail is heavier than exponential — and measured per position. A back is not a short receiver: his bulk is far shorter (S(10) .27 against a receiver's .51) while his 40-yard tail matches a tight end's, because a back's long ones are screens that break rather than routes run deep. Each receiver then gets one exponent against his own position's curve, S_player(x) = S_pos(x)^α, estimated as α = n / −Σ ln S_pos(y) from every catch rather than the rare long ones. That is what makes a 40+ number estimable when the median pass-catcher has two 40+ catches in three seasons. BPI is 1/α, read within position.
              3. Depth. One season of catch yardage says less about the next than it looks like it does: regress a receiver's α on last year's and the carryover is only 0.28. So α is shrunk hard, and what it is shrunk toward is no longer his position's average shape but his position's average shape at his depth — ADOT, measured over targets at −0.086 of log α per yard. Targets and not catches is the whole point: a catch list can only describe balls a receiver caught, so the deep threat who ran twelve go routes and caught three looks to it like a man with three catches. His air yards remember the other nine. It is a prior, never a correction applied on top of α — his catch yardage already carries his depth, and tilting it again double-counts.
              4. Volume. Two catches is two chances at a long one and eight is eight, so the rate term matters as much as the shape does — and it is a question about this Sunday, turning on a depth chart and an injury report no three-season average carries. Where the book posts a receptions line it therefore sets most of that term: the line is de-vigged and solved, not read, since 4.5 at an even price means P(5+) = ½ and the mean of a count that clears five half the time is nearer 4.7. Against week-1 actuals the posted line beat the historical rate outright (RMSE 1.98 vs 2.09), and a 70/30 blend toward it edges both. A player with no posted line keeps his history. The longest-reception ladder this page has an edge against is never read into any of it.
              5. Putting it together. λ(x) = catches/game × S_pos(x)^α × defense, player terms shrunk toward position means, defense position-specific. P(longest ≥ x) = 1 − e^−λ(x), with one global λ scale fit on a held-out season. Held out, predicted/actual lands inside 7% at every rung through 20+, where all but a handful of lines live; one shared curve missed a back's 20+ by a quarter and his 40+ by four times. A back priced deeper than 20 still reads ~20% light, so a negative edge there is likelier to be the model than the price.
              6. Edge. likelihood − 1/decimal odds against that exact line; EV = (likelihood × decimal) − 1.

              The sample gate is not decoration: ungated, the biggest edge on the board belongs to a receiver with zero career catches, where the model reports a position average rather than evidence. Those players are tagged THIN. On injuries, depth-chart changes and game script the model knows nothing and the book knows a great deal, which is why volume is now largely borrowed rather than guessed — that concession is the receptions line above. What is not borrowed is the shape of a receiver's yardage and the matchup it is measured against, and that, plus a price the model never saw, is the whole of the claim.
