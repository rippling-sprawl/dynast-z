Projection Model

How a player prop projection is built: start from what the player usually gets and how well he uses it, adjust slightly for the matchup, then check the result against the sportsbook's line.

The Formula
Volume × Efficiency × Defense × History → blend with book line
The Inputs4 factors

1Volume

Targets, carries or pass attempts per game. Recent games count more, and last season counts as 3 games.

each game back ×0.88

2Efficiency

Yards per target, catch rate and similar, pulled toward the league average until he has enough opportunities to stand on his own.

his rate + league avg

3Defense

What this defense allowed his position, compared with what those same players usually get. Heavily regressed.

capped at ±15%

4History

His past games against this defense. Kept small on purpose, because there's no evidence it predicts anything.

max 5% say
Team Context

Also adjusts volume and efficiency: teammates out (and who gets their touches), his injury tag, pace, the spread, offensive linemen out, a new starting QB.

From Number to Bet

Build a distribution

Not just one number. Rushing and receiving yards are skewed right (one long play), passing yards follow a bell curve, and counts and TDs use count distributions. The spread is wider for low-volume roles. The over/under chances come from this curve, not from his hit rate.

Respect the market

When there's a line, the model keeps 35% of its gap from the book. Big lines are shaded a few percent lower first, because the public bets overs on names it knows. Fit on 6,000+ 2025 lines: edges of 10% or more hit 58.7%.

Projection = (book line) + 0.35 × (model − book line)Constants are starting points — backtest before trusting
