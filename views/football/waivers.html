Reading a block. Bars are scaled against the top bid for that player, so the winning margin is visible even on the $1 claims. The green got him row is the claim that processed.

The high bid did not always win. Six claims were voided by Sleeper before price mattered — the manager was over budget or had no roster room. Those are hatched and tagged over budget / roster full, and did not beat the winner on money. Two more were true ties broken by waiver priority.

Bidders are counted per manager. Several managers stacked the same player across multiple claim slots; each is shown once, at their best claim. The raw per-claim rows are all in the CSV.

The team beside a player is his team today, not the one he was on when the bid was placed — Sleeper's player list only describes the present. It is a label on this page and nothing more; every number here keys on player id.

What Week 1 Should Have Cost — 2025

Thirteen claims, $1,643 of week 1 money. Below is what each player went on to score from week 2 on, what that says the bid should have been, and — the part that decides it in a chop league — whether the manager who won him was still alive to use him. One team is eliminated every week, 18 down to 2.

PlayerBidFair (pts)Fair (VOR) Over / underROS ptsDelivered ManagerChopped

Fair (pts) splits the $1,643 in proportion to raw rest-of-season points — the literal reading, and it badly flatters quarterbacks. Fair (VOR) splits it by points above replacement, where replacement is the score of the last player at that position actually started league-wide that week. That is the honest column: week 1 started eight players and exactly one quarterback, so a QB's raw total was nearly worthless at the margin. The superflex arrives in week 5, the second QB slot in week 13. Delivered is the rest-of-season total, with the green portion showing what the buyer was still alive to start — the gold remainder scored for someone who had already been chopped, or sat on a bench.
