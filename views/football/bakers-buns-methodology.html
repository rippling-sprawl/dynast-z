How the score is built

Weighted z-scores

Each input is converted to a z-score against the 32-team field, then blended. The ones that count down — O-Line and SoS ranks, the two tiers, and last season’s luck — are inverted first, so a positive z-score always means “better than average.”

Four of the seven describe an offense and its circumstances. Havoc is the one that describes a defense, which is why it carries 15% — the only look the model gets at the other side of the ball, and pressure is the stickiest thing a defense does. It is still short of the line because one pressure rate is not a defense and the rate here is a floor on the real thing. Its 15% came ten points off O-Line and five off SoS.

Coaches is the one that describes neither — the people deciding what the players run, in a year when ten teams changed head coach and 25 changed at least one coordinator. It is light for a different reason: it is one outlet’s opinion rather than a measurement, and 10% says the model has a view here, not that it knows. Its five points each came off O-Line and Rest. The line is still the heaviest input in the blend after both of them.

Luck is the last in and the lightest at 5%, taken off SoS, which drops to 15%. It is 2025 wins above Pythagorean expectation — what a team actually won minus what its points scored and allowed said it should have — and it is inverted, so the team that won fewer games than its points implied scores as the positive. That is the claim: a record ahead of its points is carried, not established, and the league’s history is that it does not repeat. It is 5% rather than more because it is a single season of a single point differential, the noisiest input here, and some real part of it — close-game coaching, a kicker — is not luck at all.

Where the inputs come from

Verified against the sources
  • O-Line — the PFF 2026 ranking, 1–32, matching all 32 rows exactly
  • SoS — Sharp Football’s odds-based strength of schedule; the five easiest and hardest match exactly
  • Rest — Sharp Football’s net rest edge in days (Bears +15 to Chargers −24)
  • OFF Tier — Action Network’s 2026 offense rankings, tiers 1–9; tier 1 is the best offense, so the tier is inverted into its z-score the way the O-Line rank is
  • Coaches — Action Network’s 2026 coaching staff rankings, head coach and both coordinators judged together. The tier is scored, not the 1–32 order: the gap between the 13th and 14th staff is a tie broken inside one bucket, while the gap between two tiers is the claim being made. Tier 1 is the best staff, so it is inverted. The eight tiers are uneven on purpose — two teams at the top, eight in tier 7 — and z-scoring keeps that
  • Luck — 2025 wins above Pythagorean expectation, from each team’s own points scored and allowed. Inverted, so a bounce-back candidate scores positive and a regression candidate scores negative; the raw gap, the record and the expected wins are all in the Inputs worksheet on the team card
  • Havoc — share of dropbacks faced ending in a sack or a QB hit, from nflverse play-by-play, blended 75% last season / 25% the season before. Higher is better, so nothing is inverted. It is a floor on pressure rather than PFF’s number — hurries are hand-charted and in no free feed — which is part of why it sits at 15% rather than level with the line

Why O-Line carries the most weight

Preseason rank vs playoffs, 2016–2025

Ten years of PFF’s preseason ranking, sorted into quartiles and counted on who actually made the playoffs:

  • Ranks 1–8 — 59% made it (the top four alone: 65%)
  • 9–16 — 48%
  • 17–24 — 33%
  • 25–32 — 26%

Edge in the chart is that quartile base rate minus what FanDuel’s price implies (8/13/26), against the raw number rather than a de-vigged one. The widest disagreements: Cardinals +28% and Jets +21% to make, Ravens +39% and Texans +35% to miss. See Graphs.

Where the win total comes from

DraftKings, FanDuel, theScore

The books do not always hang the total on the same number — DraftKings had the Cardinals at 3.5 while FanDuel and theScore had 4.5 — so the Wins column is chosen rather than copied. Of every line the three of them quote, it takes the one whose de-vigged Over price sits closest to even: the number the market cannot lean off, which is what a main line is.

Over and Under are the plain average of the books’ prices at that line with the vig left in — something to hold up against a screen, not a fair value nobody is offering. Hover a total to see which books priced it; where a market is thin it can be one.

Reading the colors

  • Green / orange on the metric columns: better or worse than the 32-team average
  • The Metrics and Odds bands above the headings mark that split: everything under Metrics is heat-mapped against the field, everything under Odds is a price and is left alone
  • The colors is a heat map, not a switch — it deepens with distance from that average, scaled to each column’s own spread, so the strongest tint in a column is that column’s most extreme team
  • Luck is heat-mapped like everything else beside it, because it is inverted before it is scored: green means they won fewer games than their points implied and are due to bounce back, orange means they won more and are due to regress. The team card puts that in words — a bounce-back or regression candidate, for anyone a full win either side of their expectation
  • Odds columns are left uncolorsed — a plus price means opposite things on Make and Miss, and the Wins line is not a price at all, so it carries no sign
