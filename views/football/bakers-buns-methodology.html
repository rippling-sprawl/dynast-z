How the score is built

Weighted z-scores

Each input is converted to a z-score against the 32-team field, then blended. O-Line and SoS ranks are inverted first, so a positive z-score always means “better than average.”

Where the inputs come from

Verified against the sources
  • O-Line — the PFF 2026 ranking, 1–32, matching all 32 rows exactly
  • SoS — Sharp Football’s odds-based strength of schedule; the five easiest and hardest match exactly
  • Rest — Sharp Football’s net rest edge in days (Bears +15 to Chargers −24)
  • Eye Test — manual, 0–10

Why O-Line carries the most weight

Preseason rank vs playoffs, 2016–2025

Ten years of PFF’s preseason ranking, sorted into quartiles and counted on who actually made the playoffs:

  • Ranks 1–8 — 59% made it (the top four alone: 65%)
  • 9–16 — 48%
  • 17–24 — 33%
  • 25–32 — 26%

Edge in the chart is that quartile base rate minus what FanDuel’s price implies (8/13/26), against the raw number rather than a de-vigged one. The widest disagreements: Cardinals +28% and Jets +21% to make, Ravens +39% and Texans +35% to miss. See Graphs.

Where the win total comes from

DraftKings, FanDuel, theScore

The books do not always hang the total on the same number — DraftKings had the Cardinals at 3.5 while FanDuel and theScore had 4.5 — so the Wins column is chosen rather than copied. Of every line the three of them quote, it takes the one whose de-vigged Over price sits closest to even: the number the market cannot lean off, which is what a main line is.

Over and Under are the plain average of the books’ prices at that line with the vig left in — something to hold up against a screen, not a fair value nobody is offering. Hover a total to see which books priced it; where a market is thin it can be one.

Reading the colors

  • Green / orange on the metric columns: better or worse than the 32-team average
  • The Metrics and Odds bands above the headings mark that split: everything under Metrics is heat-mapped against the field, everything under Odds is a price and is left alone
  • The colors is a heat map, not a switch — it deepens with distance from that average, scaled to each column’s own spread, so the strongest tint in a column is that column’s most extreme team
  • Amber on Luck: won more than their points implied, so due to regress
  • Blue on Luck: won fewer, so due to bounce back
  • Odds columns are left uncolorsed — a plus price means opposite things on Make and Miss, and the Wins line is not a price at all, so it carries no sign
