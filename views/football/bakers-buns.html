Methodology

How the score is built

Weighted z-scores

Each input is converted to a z-score against the 32-team field, then blended. O-Line and SoS ranks are inverted first, so a positive z-score always means “better than average.”

Where the inputs come from

Verified against the sources
  • O-Line — the PFF 2026 ranking, 1–32, matching all 32 rows exactly
  • SoS — Sharp Football’s odds-based strength of schedule; the five easiest and hardest match exactly
  • Rest — Sharp Football’s net rest edge in days (Bears +15 to Chargers −24)
  • Eye Test — manual, 0–10

Why O-Line carries the most weight

Preseason rank vs playoffs, 2016–2025

Ten years of PFF’s preseason ranking, sorted into quartiles and counted on who actually made the playoffs:

  • Ranks 1–8 — 59% made it (the top four alone: 65%)
  • 9–16 — 48%
  • 17–24 — 33%
  • 25–32 — 26%

Edge in the chart is that quartile base rate minus what FanDuel’s price implies (8/13/26), against the raw number rather than a de-vigged one. The widest disagreements: Cardinals +28% and Jets +21% to make, Ravens +39% and Texans +35% to miss. See Graphs.

Reading the colours

  • Green / orange on the metric columns: better or worse than the 32-team average
  • The colour is a heat map, not a switch — it deepens with distance from that average, scaled to each column’s own spread, so the strongest tint in a column is that column’s most extreme team
  • Amber on Luck: won more than their points implied, so due to regress
  • Blue on Luck: won fewer, so due to bounce back
  • Odds columns are left uncoloured — a plus price means opposite things on Make and Miss

Sources

  • PFF — 2026 offensive line rankings Feeds the 40% O-Line input

    All 32 lines graded off individual player grades weighted toward pass protection, over three years of history. Rankings move weekly with injuries; results start counting after Week 4. The per-team read is on each team’s card above.

  • Sharp Football — schedule rest disparity Feeds the 15% Rest input

    Rest edge is the extra recovery days a team has versus its opponent, summed across the season. A 3–6 day advantage has covered 54.6% since 2016 (+4.2% ROI over 233 games), while short-week road games after Week 6 win just 43.9% and cover 47.4% (−9.4% ROI). 2026’s 39-day spread from best to worst is the widest since 2000.

  • @sharpfootball — strength of schedule Feeds the 25% SoS input

    Odds-based SoS refreshed once all 32 teams were in camp. Easiest: Lions, Bengals, Saints, Jets, Ravens. Hardest: Cardinals, Dolphins, Panthers, Commanders, Cowboys — matching the sheet exactly.

  • @mrcaseb — team strength vs schedule The “Combo” chart

    Derives implied team strength (points above average on a neutral field) from all 272 DraftKings spreads, then plots each team against its average opponent strength adjusted for home field. Finds who is good, and who is good and has an easy road.

  • @MikeClayNFL — projected O-line starters O-Line

    A chart of projected 2026 starting offensive lines for all 32 teams. Useful as the depth-chart companion to PFF’s graded ranking; the post text itself is just the label.

  • @ihartitz — goal-line rush volume Rushing

    2025 team rush attempts from inside the 5. Bills led at 37, then Colts 31, Rams and Commanders 29. The Jets were last by a mile at 5, with Panthers 9 and Titans/Raiders 10 — a TD-equity signal for backfields on both ends.

  • @ihartitz — Frank Reich’s recent record Jets

    Walks through the new Jets OC’s last five years: fired midseason by the Colts in 2022 with the 30th scoring offense, then fired by Carolina after 11 games in 2023.

  • @adamlevitan — where the fired coaches landed Coaching

    Tracks previously-fired offensive coaches into their 2026 jobs: Shane Waldron to Jacksonville, Nathaniel Hackett to Arizona, Kevin Patullo to Miami, Matt Nagy to the Giants. A fade list for those offenses.

  • @lordreebs — league-wide offensive trends Context

    Offenses flattened out in 2025 after gaining ground in 2024, with the kickoff rules and a size arms race on both sides of the ball as the drivers. Framing for where scoring sits heading into 2026.

  • @wheatonbrando Low value

    Filed under Divisions & Playoffs in the doc, but the post is a throwaway Texans joke with no analytical content. Kept only so the doc’s link list stays accounted for.

  • @fball_insights — O-line consensus rankings Cross-check on the O-Line input

    Averages six preseason offensive line rankings (Clay, Sharp, 4for4, FTN, USA, FPTS) into one table with a standard deviation per team, as of 7 July. Agrees with PFF at the top (Broncos 1st, Eagles 2nd) and the bottom (Browns last), but the SD column is the useful part: the Colts, Chiefs and Jaguars carry the widest disagreement between sources, so their 40%-weighted O-Line input is the least settled.
