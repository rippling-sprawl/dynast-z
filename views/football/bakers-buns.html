Sources

  • PFF — 2026 offensive line rankings Feeds the 40% O-Line input

    All 32 lines graded off individual player grades weighted toward pass protection, over three years of history. Rankings move weekly with injuries; results start counting after Week 4. The per-team read is on each team’s card above.

  • Action Network — 2026 offensive line rankings Qualitative only — does not feed the O-Line input

    Brandon Anderson ranks all 32 lines into named tiers on four criteria: the unit over its best individual, continuity, depth and versatility, and scheme fit. Read against PFF it is a second opinion rather than a replacement — the 40% O-Line input stays PFF’s rank — and the teams the two boards disagree about are the ones worth a second look. They agree almost exactly at the top (Broncos 1st, Bills 6th, Chiefs 8th) and the bottom (Packers 28th, Jaguars 30th), so the gaps stand out: the Ravens are 15th here against PFF’s 24th, the Patriots 21st against 13th, and the Cowboys, Giants, Steelers and Raiders all move four or five spots. The Ravens and Patriots are the two teams whose 40% input would look materially different on the other board. Each team’s summary sits inside its Offensive line block above.

  • Sharp Football — schedule rest disparity Feeds the 15% Rest input

    Rest edge is the extra recovery days a team has versus its opponent, summed across the season. A 3–6 day advantage has covered 54.6% since 2016 (+4.2% ROI over 233 games), while short-week road games after Week 6 win just 43.9% and cover 47.4% (−9.4% ROI). 2026’s 39-day spread from best to worst is the widest since 2000.

  • @sharpfootball — strength of schedule Feeds the 25% SoS input

    Odds-based SoS refreshed once all 32 teams were in camp. Easiest: Lions, Bengals, Saints, Jets, Ravens. Hardest: Cardinals, Dolphins, Panthers, Commanders, Cowboys — matching the sheet exactly.

  • DraftKings, FanDuel & theScore — regular-season win totals Feeds the Wins, Over and Under columns

    The three books rarely hang the total on the same number, so the line in the table is chosen rather than copied: of everything they quote, it is the line whose de-vigged Over price sits closest to even — the number the market cannot lean off. Over and Under are the plain average of the books’ prices at that line, vig left in, so they are prices to compare against a screen rather than a fair value nobody offers. Hover a total to see which books made it. Built by scripts/build_bun_win_totals.py from the same captures the Odds page reads, so re-importing a book refreshes the column.

  • @mrcaseb — team strength vs schedule The “Combo” chart

    Derives implied team strength (points above average on a neutral field) from all 272 DraftKings spreads, then plots each team against its average opponent strength adjusted for home field. Finds who is good, and who is good and has an easy road.

  • @MikeClayNFL — projected O-line starters O-Line

    A chart of projected 2026 starting offensive lines for all 32 teams. Useful as the depth-chart companion to PFF’s graded ranking; the post text itself is just the label.

  • @ihartitz — goal-line rush volume Rushing

    2025 team rush attempts from inside the 5. Bills led at 37, then Colts 31, Rams and Commanders 29. The Jets were last by a mile at 5, with Panthers 9 and Titans/Raiders 10 — a TD-equity signal for backfields on both ends.

  • @ihartitz — Frank Reich’s recent record Jets

    Walks through the new Jets OC’s last five years: fired midseason by the Colts in 2022 with the 30th scoring offense, then fired by Carolina after 11 games in 2023.

  • @adamlevitan — where the fired coaches landed Coaching

    Tracks previously-fired offensive coaches into their 2026 jobs: Shane Waldron to Jacksonville, Nathaniel Hackett to Arizona, Kevin Patullo to Miami, Matt Nagy to the Giants. A fade list for those offenses.

  • nflverse — 2025 play-by-play Feeds four of the five Stats & Regression tiles

    Every regular-season play of 2025 as a table, which is the only free source that carries qb_hit — and pressure is the one number in that block that repeats, so the block does not work without it. scripts/fetch_nfl_regression.py makes one pass over the file for havoc rate, turnover margin, the one-score record and fourth-down conversions, and writes data/nfl_regression_2026.json. A pressure here is a dropback ending in a sack or a hit: hurries are charted by hand and are in no free feed, so the rate is a floor rather than PFF’s number. Turnovers are counted from scrimmage only, a one-score game is decided by eight or fewer, and fourth down counts the plays a team chose to run rather than punts and field goals.

  • Over The Cap — salary cap space Feeds the Dead cap tile

    2026 cap charged to players who are no longer on the roster, scraped from the same page’s first table by the script above. It is the odd one out in that block — dead money does not regress, it is spent — and it is carried because it answers the question the other four raise: whether a team that got unlucky has the room to do anything about it. The league pays about $44M a head, but the mean is nearly meaningless here: the spread runs from Seattle’s $0.7M to Miami’s $179M.

  • Wikipedia — current NFL head coaches Feeds the Coaches block

    With the offensive and defensive coordinator lists, the whole of each team’s Coaches block. Ten teams changed head coach for 2026 and 25 changed at least one coordinator, so the tenure year is the number to read first. The coordinator lists carry a previous-position column and the head coach list does not, which is why only coordinators show where they came from.

  • @lordreebs — league-wide offensive trends Context

    Offenses flattened out in 2025 after gaining ground in 2024, with the kickoff rules and a size arms race on both sides of the ball as the drivers. Framing for where scoring sits heading into 2026.

  • @wheatonbrando Low value

    Filed under Divisions & Playoffs in the doc, but the post is a throwaway Texans joke with no analytical content. Kept only so the doc’s link list stays accounted for.

  • @fball_insights — O-line consensus rankings Cross-check on the O-Line input

    Averages six preseason offensive line rankings (Clay, Sharp, 4for4, FTN, USA, FPTS) into one table with a standard deviation per team, as of 7 July. Agrees with PFF at the top (Broncos 1st, Eagles 2nd) and the bottom (Browns last), but the SD column is the useful part: the Colts, Chiefs and Jaguars carry the widest disagreement between sources, so their 40%-weighted O-Line input is the least settled.
