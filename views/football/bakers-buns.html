How the score is built

Weighted z-scores

Each input is converted to a z-score against the 32-team field, then blended. O-Line and SoS ranks are inverted first, so a positive z-score always means “better than average.”

      Rest edge — what Sharp actually found

      Feeds the 15% Rest input

      Rest edge is the extra days of recovery a team has versus its opponent; net rest sums that across the season.

      • A 3–6 day rest advantage has covered at 54.6% since 2016 — +4.2% ROI over 233 games
      • Short-week road games after Week 6 are the trap: 43.9% wins, 47.4% ATS, −9.4% ROI
      • Those same road teams with extra rest instead covered 53.3% — an 11.2-point swing
      • 2026’s 39-day spread from best to worst is the widest since 2000

      Most helped: Bears +15, Bills +14, then Cowboys, Titans, Cardinals. Most hurt: Chargers −24, Eagles −15, Dolphins, Steelers, Chiefs.

      Sources

      What each link actually says, so the page stands on its own.

      • PFF — 2026 offensive line rankings Feeds the 40% O-Line input

        All 32 lines graded off individual player grades weighted toward pass protection, over three years of history. Rankings move weekly with injuries; results start counting after Week 4. Summarized in full in the O-Line section.

      • Sharp Football — schedule rest disparity Feeds the 15% Rest input

        Quantifies net rest edge and its betting effect: 3–6 days of extra rest covers 54.6% since 2016, while short-week road games after Week 6 cover just 47.4%. 2026 has the widest spread since 2000.

      • @sharpfootball — strength of schedule Feeds the 25% SoS input

        Odds-based SoS refreshed once all 32 teams were in camp. Easiest: Lions, Bengals, Saints, Jets, Ravens. Hardest: Cardinals, Dolphins, Panthers, Commanders, Cowboys — matching the sheet exactly.

      • @mrcaseb — team strength vs schedule The “Combo” chart

        Derives implied team strength (points above average on a neutral field) from all 272 DraftKings spreads, then plots each team against its average opponent strength adjusted for home field. Finds who is good, and who is good and has an easy road.

      • @MikeClayNFL — projected O-line starters O-Line

        A chart of projected 2026 starting offensive lines for all 32 teams. Useful as the depth-chart companion to PFF’s graded ranking above; the post text itself is just the label.

      • @ihartitz — goal-line rush volume Rushing

        2025 team rush attempts from inside the 5. Bills led at 37, then Colts 31, Rams and Commanders 29. The Jets were last by a mile at 5, with Panthers 9 and Titans/Raiders 10 — a TD-equity signal for backfields on both ends.

      • @ihartitz — Frank Reich’s recent record Supports the Jets under

        Walks through the new Jets OC’s last five years: fired midseason by the Colts in 2022 with the 30th scoring offense, then fired by Carolina after 11 games in 2023. Direct support for the Jets win-total unders already logged in Bets.

        Tracks previously-fired offensive coaches into their 2026 jobs: Shane Waldron to Jacksonville, Nathaniel Hackett to Arizona, Kevin Patullo to Miami, Matt Nagy to the Giants. A fade list for those offenses.
