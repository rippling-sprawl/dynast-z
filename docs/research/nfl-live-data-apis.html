Live NFL box scores and odds,
priced against a Sunday.

Sixteen providers, evaluated against one workload: an unattended pipeline that polls from 8:00am to 11:55pm ET on Sundays, under $100 a month, with no human in the loop. Every price, quota and limit below traces to a dated primary source; where a claim survived only one of three adversarial checks it is marked as unresolved rather than stated flat.

The short version

The field does not narrow on price. It narrows on two things almost nobody publishes on a pricing page: whether the cheap tier is actually live, and whether the terms of service let you store what you pull.

Recommendation

balldontlie GOAT, $39.99/month, for both feeds on one bill. It is the only option that clears every constraint on published terms: a static-key header over plain GET, 600 requests per minute with no monthly cap, live in-game data that is not tier-gated, and a data licence that expressly permits caching, storing and building derivative databases for “lawful sportsbook and wagering products”. At $9.99 the ALL-STAR tier already covers score, team stats and player stats — step up to GOAT only if you need the game clock, live possession, or odds.

Pair it with ESPN for free opening and closing lines, and with The Odds API at $59/month if you need multi-book in-play prices at 40-second resolution. Both carry caveats worth reading before you commit — ESPN's are legal, The Odds API's are arithmetic.

What actually eliminated services

Price was rarely the reason. SportsDataIO publishes a $99 NFL tier — inside the ceiling — but caps it at 100 calls a day and ships every Discovery Lab feed next-day. API-SPORTS has a free tier whose NFL odds are pre-match only, refreshed four times daily. MySportsFeeds labels every published tier “Non-Live access”. nflverse deliberately refuses to publish a season containing an unfinished game. Pro Football Reference blocks at 20 requests a minute and has stated it will never offer an API. Three of the named candidates — Pikkit, VegasInsider and Action Network — have no programmatic access at all, and JuiceReel's API, which does exist, returns only the connected user's own bet slips.

The Sunday shape

Every quota question depends on one fact: a Sunday is not a solid block of football. It is four kickoff waves with dead air between them, and the number of games running at once swings from one to ten. Polling flat across the stated window bills you for the gaps.

The arithmetic, shown

Score and period at 25s across the full window is 57,300 ÷ 25 = 2,292 calls — one scoreboard call covers the whole slate, so games do not multiply it. Player stats at 2 minutes is 478 snapshots, but the box score is a per-game endpoint, so with 14 concurrent games that is 478 × 14 = 6,692. Team stats need no separate call on any provider reviewed — they arrive in the same box-score response, so the tighter cadence binds. Naive total: 8,984 per Sunday, ~38,600 per month across 4.3 Sundays.

Gate the poller on games actually in progress and it falls to 3,440 per Sunday — ~14,800 a month. Both figures appear in the tables below, because the naive one is what a vendor quota has to survive if the pipeline is written the obvious way.

The number that does the filtering

9.4 requests per minute at peak. That is trivially inside balldontlie's 60/min, Sportradar's 1 QPS, and OpticOdds' 2,500-per-15s. It is outside API-SPORTS' free 10/min once you account for retries, and it is more than 34× SportsDataIO's 100 calls per day. Monthly totals matter less than this one figure.

On ESPN, which is free and very good

Measured on this machine today: 100 concurrent GETs returned 100/100 at 67.8 requests per second with zero throttling — roughly 430× the peak this workload needs. The /summary box score was confirmed populating during a live game, with per-athlete stat lines across ten categories. Nothing else here is both that complete and that free.

Two things stop it being the recommendation. Propagation measured 26–33 seconds from ESPN's own play timestamp to the feed — just outside the 25s target, and that is measured against ESPN's clock, so true lag behind the broadcast is larger. And the Disney Terms of Use that govern ESPN prohibit, in three separate clauses, automated access by “robot, spider, script”, “compiling, building, creating or contributing to any collection of data, data set or database”, and any “commercial or business-related use … whether or not for profit”. There is no technical barrier at all. The barrier is contractual, and it is the same class of objection that disqualified Pro Football Reference — so it is flagged here rather than quietly discounted.

The Odds API bills multiplicatively — model it before you subscribe

Cost is markets × regions, not calls. Moneyline + spread + total in the us region is 3 credits per call, not 1. Books convert to region-equivalents at 10:1, and the US books are split across two billable keys — reaching both DraftKings and theScore Bet needs regions=us,us2, which doubles every call to 6 credits.

Featured-market polling at 40s lands around 17,000–43,000 credits a month, comfortably inside the $59 / 100k tier. Player props do not fit: they are per-event only, so 14 games × 5 prop markets at 60-second cadence is roughly 67,200 credits in a single Sunday. Poll props on a slow cadence or not at all.

Closing line value is a storage problem, not a feed problem

The Odds API's historical endpoints carry a 10× multiplier and are paid-only. balldontlie states outright that it does not store historical prop data. ESPN gives you opening and closing lines for free, from DraftKings, in the same scoreboard payload the pipeline already fetches. If CLV is the goal, snapshot the line yourself on a schedule — buying history back later is the expensive path on every provider here.

Method & sources

Two parallel deep-research runs (203 agents) established the vendor landscape; a third round (18 agents) closed the gaps the first two flagged as unevidenced. Load-bearing price and quota claims were re-fetched by independent adversarial verifiers instructed to refute them.

What was measured here, not read

ESPN's rate ceiling, keylessness, and live box-score population; the 26–33s propagation figure; Pro Football Reference's Cloudflare challenge; the 401/403 auth probes on The Odds API, SportsDataIO, Sportradar, API-SPORTS, balldontlie, SportsGameOdds and api.nfl.com; nflverse's release timestamps; and every colour and font in the vibe cards.

Known limits of this report

Four vendors publish no price at all — Sportradar, Stats Perform, OpticOdds and the NFL — and no figure has been invented for them. balldontlie's actual latency was not measured, because it needs a key; its 48-hour GOAT trial would settle it against a live slate, and that is the one test worth running before committing. SportsGameOdds' paid pricing remains unresolved: three of four claims about it failed verification. Design vibes are reconstructed from stylesheets and brand assets rather than screenshots, since no headless browser was available; cards for login-gated products are built from the public marketing site and labelled.
