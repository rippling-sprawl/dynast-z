# NFL Player-vs-Defense Matchup Visualizations: Analytics Community and Media Chart Designs

Scope note: these notes cover designs from analysts, media and the open-source community, not commercial betting or fantasy apps. 4for4 and PFF's fantasy charts are paid fantasy products. They are included only as reference points because they are the clearest documented examples of a design pattern. Data requirements are marked **[BOX]** if the chart can be built from per-game box-score logs (yards, receptions, targets, carries, TDs by game and opponent), **[PBP]** if it needs play-by-play (nflfastR-style fields such as air_yards, pass_location, epa), and **[CHART/TRACK]** if it needs coverage charting (PFF, Fantasy Points Data) or player tracking (Next Gen Stats).

## How analysts show a defense's weakness against a position or player type

### Takeaway
Three designs dominate. (1) Opponent-adjusted "allowed vs. expected" values, encoded as rank-banded colors in a team-by-week grid. (2) Field-zone heat maps (3 depths x 3 directions) showing EPA/CPOE allowed per zone. (3) Matchup tables that pair offense rank against defense rank with a text "edge" label. Of the three, only the opponent-adjusted position grid can be built purely from box scores. Zone heat maps need play-by-play, and coverage-split charts (man/zone, single-high/two-high) need charting data.

### Cited Findings
- **Schedule-adjusted fantasy points allowed heat grid (4for4 "Hot Spots," fantasy reference point).** There is one grid per position (QB/RB/WR/TE/K/DEF, plus PPR variants). Each cell shows the week's opponent and that opponent's average *schedule-adjusted* fantasy points allowed to the position, with schedule bias removed for an "apples-to-apples" comparison. Color is rank-banded: red = very strong opponent (ranks 1–5 vs. position), pink = 6–10, light green = 23–27, green = 28–32, and neutral in between. Summary columns average opponent strength over the fantasy playoff weeks (e.g., "PO2" = Weeks 15–16) and the rest of the season. — [4for4 Hot Spots (adjusted)](https://www.4for4.com/recent-report/hot_spots_ff_points_adjusted); [4for4 team-level SOS by position](https://www.4for4.com/reports/2017/w8/hot-spots-team-level-sos-position). **[BOX]**: fantasy points by position by game by opponent come straight from box scores.
- **Opponent-adjustment formula (Open Source Football, Jonathan Goldberg).** `adj_off = off_epa + (league_mean − opp_def_avg)` and `adj_def = def_epa + (league_mean − opp_off_avg)`. The opponent average is a 10-game simple moving average lagged one week, so only prior games count, and the league mean is also a lagged 10-game moving average. Week 1 adjustment = 0. Adjusted EPA predicted wins at 0.640 accuracy vs. 0.635 unadjusted, which the author calls a "slight edge." The article shows side-by-side unadjusted vs. adjusted team plots. — [OSF: Adjusting EPA for Strength of Opponent (Aug 2020)](https://www.opensourcefootball.com/posts/2020-08-20-adjusting-epa-for-strenght-of-opponent/). The method is metric-agnostic: substituting "fantasy points allowed to WRs" or "receiving yards allowed to TEs" for EPA turns it into a **[BOX]** "allowed vs. expected" adjustment.
- **Defense vs. the offense's WR1 (OSF, Jonas Trostle, July 2021).** WR1 is defined within team-season as the WR with the most targeted air yards (the author notes receptions or other measures could substitute). The chart compares mean EPA allowed on WR1 targets vs. on all other WR targets for each defense. It is a scatter with team logos/colors, and better defenses sit toward the top right. A coda plots every team-season since 1999. No opponent adjustment is applied; the author lists adjusting for the quality of receivers faced as future work. — [OSF: Evaluating defenses by how well they play the offense's WR1](https://www.opensourcefootball.com/posts/2021-07-30-evauluating-defenses-by-how-well-they-play-the-offenses-wr-1/). The original is **[PBP]** (EPA per target), but a **[BOX]** analogue works: define WR1 by season targets or yards, then plot each defense's yards/receptions allowed to opponents' WR1 vs. WR2/3 relative to those receivers' season averages.
- **Coverage heat maps by field zone (Kabir Devgun, Intellect Sports Journal, Jan 17, 2026).** A 3x3 grid: pass location (Left/Middle/Right) x depth (Short ≤10 air yards, Medium 11–20, Deep 21+). Each zone shows CPOE allowed, EPA/play allowed (EPA includes YAC), and the attempt count so readers can judge sample size. It answers "where on the field is this defense beatable, and how efficient are throws there." It follows the same author's QB heat-map piece. — [NFL Advanced Coverage heat maps](https://kabirdevgun.substack.com/p/nfl-advanced-coverage-heat-maps). **[PBP]**: needs air_yards and pass_location, which nflfastR provides. Box scores cannot support it.
- **OSF visualization precedents for location heat maps:** "NFL Pass Location Visualization" (Ethan Douglas, Aug 2020), "Faceted and Animated Heatmaps" (Analytics Darkweb, Aug 2020), and "Player Density and Completion Surface Estimates" (Ethan Douglas, Aug 2020). — [Open Source Football index](https://www.opensourcefootball.com/). These are older than the preferred 2022+ window but remain the canonical open-source code templates. **[PBP]**.
- **Sharp Football Analysis weekly matchup table (Oct 20, 2026, live product).** One row per offense-vs-defense pairing, listed from both sides. Columns hold offense rank, defense rank and a text edge label ("Big offense edge" / "Offense edge" / "Even" / "Defense edge" / "Big defense edge") for five areas: overall, passing, rushing, pressure and explosives (15+ yd pass / 10+ yd rush). Ranks blend success rate and EPA, weighted toward success rate because it is more stable. Last season's stats are blended in as a regressed prior to damp early-season outliers, and the ranks are opponent-adjusted. Rows expand to show trend lines. — [Sharp Football: Week 7 NFL Matchups](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/?p=129218). Data note: the page title says Week 7 but the intro text says Week 6. **[PBP]** as built, though the "rank vs. rank + edge label" encoding transfers to box-score metrics.
- **Sharp Football man/zone coverage table:** each team's man rate, zone rate, and middle-of-field closed/open rates. The snippet did not show a season or date. — [Sharp Football man/zone](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/?p=111753). **[CHART]**.
- **Player-vs-coverage scatter (Football Insights Substack, Dec 30, 2023).** X = the opponent's season rate of a coverage type (man, zone, single-high MOF-closed, two-high MOF-open). Y = the player's production against that coverage (QB fantasy points/dropback, WR/TE yards per route run). The design combines "what will the defense do" with "how does this player do against it" in one view. Data comes from the Fantasy Points Data Suite plus nflfastR. — [Week 17 Player Matchups and Data Visualizations](https://fballinsights.substack.com/p/week-17-player-matchups-and-data). **[CHART]**.
- **PFF WR vs. CB matchup chart (fantasy reference point).** It is split into three zones: left WR vs. right CB, right WR vs. left CB, and slot vs. slot CB. Columns show alignment %, targets/route, fantasy points/route and PFF grade, and a middle column marks which side has the "advantage" based solely on fantasy points per route. Expected shadow coverage was once marked with ALL CAPS names. — [PFF WR vs. CB Matchup Chart](https://www.pff.com/news/wr-vs-cb-matchup-chart); [PFF AAF Week 2 chart](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-matchups-wrcb-chart-for-aaf-week-2-2019). **[CHART]**: needs alignment and route data.
- **SumerSports team defense tables:** EPA/play, total EPA, success rate, and pass/rush splits by team. Lower defensive EPA allowed is better. — [SumerSports team defense](https://sumersports.com/teams/defensive/). **[PBP]**.
- **nfelo EPA tiers:** a team scatter of offensive vs. defensive EPA/play (the classic "EPA tiers" chart popularized in the rbsdm/nflfastR community). — [nfelo EPA tiers](https://nfeloapp.com/nfl-power-ratings/nfl-epa-tiers). **[PBP]**.

### Inferences
- The 4for4 grid and the Goldberg adjustment together give a fully box-score-buildable "defense weakness" chart: compute each defense's fantasy points (or yards/receptions/TDs) allowed per position per game, subtract what those offenses averaged against everyone else (the lagged average of the offenses faced), and render the residual as a diverging-color position x week (or position x defense) heat map.
- Zone, route, coverage and alignment views ("EPA allowed by target area," man/zone splits, WR/CB) are out of reach without at least play-by-play. Of these, only the 3x3 depth/direction grid is achievable from free nflfastR data. Coverage and alignment need paid charting.
- The Sharp "edge label" text encoding is a low-cost, accessible complement to color. It reads well on mobile and does not rely on hue alone.

### Gaps
- I found no rbsdm.com page documentation in search. Its current chart offerings (e.g., team EPA tiers, defensive splits) could not be verified. A 2021 article cited RBSDM defensive EPA rankings — [TheLines, 2021](https://www.thelines.com/nfl-defense-stats-week-3-2021/).
- I could not retrieve chart images for The Athletic, ESPN Analytics or Next Gen Stats defense-vs-position graphics. The NGS matchup previews I found (separation by alignment, shadow-coverage rates) date from 2016–2017. — [NFL.com NGS preview, Rishard Matthews vs. Jalen Ramsey](https://nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-preview-rishard-matthews-vs-jalen-ramsey-0ap3000000761729). These would be **[TRACK]**.

## How player rolling form and role trends are shown

### Takeaway
The open-source community has documented rolling-average methodology more thoroughly than chart design. The best-documented finding is that a season-to-date (expanding) or exponentially weighted window beats a fixed 10-game window. Rolling form lines, share-over-time lines and distribution plots of game outcomes can all be built from box scores, apart from snap share and route share.

### Cited Findings
- **Rolling averages of EPA (OSF, Jack Lichtenstein, Dec 2020).** The article compares simple, weighted, running and exponential moving averages, and a static 10-game window vs. a dynamic 10–20 game window. Findings: the dynamic window that uses all available games predicts better than a static 10-game window. Exponential averages work best for offensive pass/total EPA, and running averages work best for point differential and rushing. Opponent-adjusting defensive EPA *slightly reduced* predictive power. The author concludes "weighting recent performance more heavily increases a metric's predictive strength." Charts include team trajectory panels per conference showing offensive/defensive efficiency across the season. — [OSF: Exploring Rolling Averages of EPA](https://www.opensourcefootball.com/posts/2020-12-29-exploring-rolling-averages-of-epa/). The source used **[PBP]**, but the method applies directly to **[BOX]** stats (rolling yards, targets, carries).
- **Expected fantasy points for receivers (OSF, Anthony Reinhard, Aug 2020)** and **Visualizing TRAP Backs (OSF, Sam Hoppen, Aug 2020)** are role-quality views of the opportunity a player receives, not just output. — [Open Source Football index](https://www.opensourcefootball.com/). These are **[PBP]** (expected points per target use air yards and field position).
- **QB volatility with Gini coefficients (OSF, Joseph Chernak, Aug 2021)** is a game-to-game consistency measure, the dispersion counterpart to a rolling mean. — [Open Source Football index](https://www.opensourcefootball.com/). Computable from **[BOX]** per-game totals.
- **nflplotR** supplies the community's standard visual vocabulary: team logos as ggplot geoms (with correct aspect ratio and transparency), logo axis labels (`element_nfl_logo()`), and gt table integration. Version 1.6.0 is on CRAN. — [nflplotR site](https://nflplotr.nflverse.com/); [nflplotR reference manual](https://cloud.r-project.org/web/packages/nflplotR/refman/nflplotR.html). A 2024 tutorial builds an offense vs. defense EPA "diamond plot" with logos from nflreadr data — [The F5: How To Diamond Plots in R](https://thef5.substack.com/p/how-to-diamond-plots-in-r).

### Inferences
- Box-score-buildable form designs: (a) a per-game bar or dot strip with an overlaid exponential or season-to-date rolling line (per the OSF finding, prefer EWMA or expanding over a fixed short window); (b) target share and carry share by week (player targets ÷ team targets, both available in box scores) as a line or stacked area; (c) a beeswarm or ridgeline of per-game outcomes (e.g., receiving yards) with the upcoming prop line or season median marked; (d) a Gini or standard-deviation "consistency" badge.
- Snap share, route participation and air-yard share require snap counts or play-by-play. Snap counts are published separately (e.g., by nflverse) but are not in a pure box-score log.

### Gaps
- I found no documented 2022–2026 ridgeline or beeswarm game-outcome charts from the named analysts (Ben Baldwin, Kevin Cole, Arjun Menon, Tej Seth). Searches returned no primary sources for their specific chart designs. Their work on X/Bluesky is image-based and not indexed in a way search could surface.
- Kevin Cole (Unexpected Points / Predictive Football) appears only as a podcast guest discussing a points-added model — [The Power Rank podcast](https://thepowerrank.libsyn.com/kevin-cole-on-points-added-for-nfl-players). His chart designs could not be verified.

## Notable matchup-preview graphics from named outlets and analysts

### Takeaway

Open Source Football's archive is mostly 2020–2021, apart from a 2025 draft value chart by Ben Baldwin. PFF's documented matchup chart is the WR/CB table.

### Cited Findings
- Sharp Football weekly matchup rankings (2026, opponent-adjusted, regressed prior, 5 categories, text edge labels, expandable trend lines) — [Sharp Football](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/?p=129218).
- Open Source Football's most recent posts are "NFL Draft Value Chart" (Ben Baldwin, May 14, 2025) and "Linguistic Analysis of NFL Prospect Reports" (July 2023). Most matchup- and defense-related posts date from 2020–2021. — [Open Source Football](https://www.opensourcefootball.com/).
- PFF WR/CB matchup chart (alignment-based zones, advantage column) — [PFF](https://www.pff.com/news/wr-vs-cb-matchup-chart); PFF also publishes weekly man/zone coverage analysis — [PFF Week 3 man/zone](https://pff.com/news/week-3-man-zone-coverage-analysis).
- NGS matchup previews (historic): where CBs and WRs line up, separation at target (e.g., "4.4 yards of separation on his eight targets"), and shadow-coverage share (e.g., a CB covering a WR on 16 of 48 passing plays, 33.3%) — [NFL.com NGS Week 11 fantasy matchups](https://www.nfl.com/news/week-11-next-gen-stats-fantasy-football-matchups-0ap3000000741511); [NGS preview Cardinals vs. Seahawks](https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-preview-cardinals-vs-seahawks-0ap3000000761741). **[TRACK]**.
- FiveThirtyEight built a QB matchup metric from ESPN Stats & Info data — [FiveThirtyEight: It's hard to measure NFL matchups](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/its-hard-to-measure-nfl-matchups-so-we-built-a-better-metric/).

- A Big Data Bowl student Shiny app includes a radar comparing a defender's attributes to position benchmarks — [NYC Data Science: Tackling NFL Defensive Data with R Shiny](https://nycdatascience.com/blog/student-works/r-shiny/tackling-nfl-defensive-data-with-r-shiny). **[TRACK]**.

### Inferences
- In this research, the NFL graphics with documented designs are mostly tables with rank bands (Sharp, PFF, 4for4) or nflplotR logo scatters. Rich chart forms (zone heat maps, coverage scatters) appear mainly with independent analysts.

## Percentile-bar player cards (Baseball Savant, StatsBomb/FBref) and NFL transfer

### Takeaway
*Cross-sport inspiration.* Savant's red/blue percentile slider bars and StatsBomb/FBref radars and pizzas are mature patterns with documented scaling conventions. They transfer to NFL matchup context if the percentile reference group is position-specific. A matchup version can pair the player's percentile bar with the opponent defense's percentile "allowed to position" on the same row.

### Cited Findings
- **Baseball Savant (MLB, cross-sport).** Percentile rankings are "front-and-center" on player pages, with slider bars restyled in the 2023 redesign — [MLB.com: new-look Statcast player pages](https://www.mlb.com/news/baseball-savant-statcast-player-pages-new-look). Color reading: deeper red = closer to the 100th percentile, deeper blue = closer to the 1st — [Razzball](https://razzball.com/the-xfiles-tangled-up-in-red-and-blue/) (third-party description). The July 2026 Player Comparison Tool compares multiple players' percentile rankings in the player-page style and can highlight the "winner" of each metric — [Savant changelog: Player Comparison Tool](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/changelog/2026-07-20-player-comparison-tool); there is also a percentile rankings leaderboard with filters — [Savant percentile leaderboard](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/percentile-rankings).
- **StatsBomb radar (soccer, cross-sport).** StatsBomb "popularised" radars for player stats. By convention, each axis's boundaries are the top 5% and bottom 5% of production by players *at that position*. — [mplsoccer radar docs](https://mplsoccer.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gallery/radar/plot_radar.html). mplsoccer also implements percentile pizza (Nightingale) plots, including comparison variants — [mplsoccer gallery](https://mplsoccer.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gallery/index.html).
- **NFL percentile context in practice.** SumerSports position pages publish "Percentile Benchmarks (2022–2025)" alongside stat definitions — [SumerSports RB page](https://sumersports.com/players/running-back/?teams=ARZ). I could not confirm whether its player pages render percentile bars.

### Inferences
- The "winner highlight" in Savant's comparison tool is the closest cross-sport analogue to a player-vs-defense card. Each row would show the player's percentile (e.g., targets/game, yards/reception among WRs) and the defense's percentile for the matching allowed stat (WR targets allowed per game, opponent-adjusted), with the larger edge highlighted. **[BOX]** for volume and efficiency stats.
- NFL adaptation issues: small samples (17 games vs. 162 or 38) make percentiles noisy early in the season. That argues for showing the games played and blending in a prior, as Sharp does. Position-specific reference pools, the StatsBomb convention, are essential because WR and TE distributions differ.
- Radars and pizzas suit fixed multi-metric profiles but compare poorly across two entities. Paired horizontal percentile bars, Savant-style, are easier to read on mobile for a matchup.

### Gaps
- Savant's exact hex colors, midpoint and bar-length mapping are not officially documented in the sources found.
- I found no primary documentation of FBref's specific pizza styling, and FBref's StatsBomb-sourced advanced data availability has changed over time. This was not verified here.
- Cleaning the Glass and NBA shot-chart/matchup designs (suggested cross-sport sources) were not researched within the tool budget.
